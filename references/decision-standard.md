# Objection Admission, Severity and Verdict Standard

## 1. Self-skepticism gate

Every candidate objection undergoes this sequence; failures are rejected, narrowed, or kept only as clearly marked unresolved questions.

| Gate | Admission question | Reject or downgrade when |
|---|---|---|
| Charitable target | Is the strongest fair reading of the quoted original claim the one under attack? | It is a weaker paraphrase, false dichotomy, or invented position |
| Materiality | If fully true, would a reasonable decision-maker change an action, condition, scope, or claim? | It is cosmetic or irrelevant to the decision |
| Causal specificity | Does this objection explain a credible failure path particular to this case? | The same vague doubt would apply equally to the opposite thesis |
| Epistemic credibility | Is there a credible basis for the objection and a way to test it? | It is only a remote possibility masquerading as a discovered flaw |
| Fair rebuttal | Has the strongest available defense been checked, including whether safeguards exist now? | A documented counter defeats the objection or mitigation is adequate for the specified risk |

The gate is not a popularity vote. A missing critical fact may remain a **blocking uncertainty** even without evidence proving the feared failure. Label it as such; do not claim the failure occurred.

Record rejected candidates honestly with reasons; zero rejections is permitted. Do not manufacture an objection simply so you can show that it was rejected. Re-examine clusters only if a **single demonstrable shared failure mechanism** emerges; do not re-admit the same weak objections by summing them.

## 2. Sound versus Safe

Classify findings with one or both:

- **Sound:** empirical or logical correctness, calculation, causation, evidence, internal consistency.
- **Safe:** robustness of the decision in the presence of adversarial questioning, irreversible harm, communication exposure, reversibility, approval and responsibility.

A sound claim may still justify an unsafe action. A defensible narrative may still be factually unsupported. Name which failure matters.

## 3. Severity without invented precision

Score each surviving finding **Load / Credibility / Cost**, each Low / Medium / High:

- **Load:** whether the issue breaks a decisive premise or merely a minor detail.
- **Credibility:** the evidence that the objection is real, rather than speculative; High does not mean certainty.
- **Cost:** consequences of taking the decision while the issue remains, including reversibility.

Order the mapping:

1. **Critical:** Load High, Credibility at least Medium, Cost at least Medium.
2. **Major:** any two axes High not already classified Critical.
3. **Moderate:** exactly one axis High.
4. **Minor:** no axis High.

A Critical finding is not automatically an unconditional STOP: if a narrow, verifiable fix removes the mechanism without destroying the proposition, **REVISE** is valid. If redesign is necessary, **RETHINK**. If no realistic correction is available and the decision cannot safely proceed, **STOP**.

Treat materially unresolved load-bearing **uncertainty** separately from proven flaws. Mark CONDITIONAL, require evidence, and choose REVISE or RETHINK when a decision must not proceed before verification.

Do not inflate severity based on the number of findings. If two non-material weaknesses interact, describe the *new interaction mechanism* and assess it directly, rather than stacking rhetorical points.

## 4. Verdict logic

| Label | Operational meaning |
|---|---|
| PROCEED | Best adversarial case fails to move the decision materially; state its falsifier and monitor |
| REVISE | Direction remains credible with specific changes or safeguards required before execution |
| RETHINK | A central premise or alternative needs investigation or redesign before commitment |
| STOP | A material, credible and unfixable/decisive flaw makes the proposed action unjustifiable |

Apply `CONDITIONAL on <claim>` when an essential fact was not verified. An external check that was impossible should not magically turn a flaw into a defect or erase the uncertainty.

For a close call, require stronger evidence for irreversible public or safety-critical decisions than for bounded, reversible internal experiments. A tiny, measurable pilot often beats endless debate, but not when the pilot itself creates unacceptable exposure.

## 5. Required falsifier and exit criteria

Even after PROCEED, identify the fact that could force reconsideration and where to check it. For REVISE, state acceptance criteria and which changes require confirmation. For RETHINK, choose the discriminating evidence needed to make a new decision. For STOP, specify why mitigation or smaller scope does not solve the issue.

A good recommendation has an explicit next action, a decision consequence, and, for high-impact changes, a rollback trigger. Never confuse a recommendation to verify with verification already completed.
